How will copyright laws work in an AI world? Who owns the IP if an AI agent invented something or contributed significantly to ideation/implementation?

Seems like I am not the only one who doesn't have this figured out yet. Part 2 of the US Copyright Office's [*Copyright and Artificial Intelligence* report ](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)published last year concluded that "...existing legal doctrines are adequate and appropriate to resolve questions of
copyrightability. Copyright law has long adapted to new technology and can enable case-by-
case determinations as to whether AI-generated outputs reflect sufficient human contribution to
warrant copyright protection." Despite this assertion from our government, a quick query to Claude concluded that this is mostly uncharted territory with a lot of grey area (link to my chatbot convo [here](https://claude.ai/share/70c1e27a-d9bd-4bfe-8b7a-12c3e1f85672)). 

Based on Claude's response and quick read of the Copyright Office's conclusion (I confess to not reading the entire report), the issue hinges on how we will define/test "sufficient human creative contribution", since only *human* creativity is copyrightable. I suspect there will be a lot of messy lawsuits before we get this figured out. Given the power and potential of AI, I think that's a trade-off I'm happy to make.
