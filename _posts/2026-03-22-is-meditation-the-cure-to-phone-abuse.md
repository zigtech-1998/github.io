Was listening to [Ep. 390 of Cal Newport's *Deep Questions* podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qSiiatD_tIg) while commuting last week. The beginning of the show samples some YouTuber's experiences with phone cleanses. Each cites an experience of increased mental presence, decreased anxiety, and a feeling of "waking up" to how uncomplicated and beautiful life can be if you simply pay attention. These YouTuber's describe the benefits of going *sans*-phone exactly the same way Sam Harris describes the benefits of meditation in his *Waking Up* meditation course. During [the first intro session](https://dynamic.wakingup.com/course/C457FA?source=content%20share&share_id=B1B037CF&pack=PKA69AD&code=SC49A5E8B), Sam describes the difference in subjective experience in meditators vs non-meditators by using the following analogy:

> "It really is difficult to exaggerate the difference between having g recognized the sky of the mind.... and never having looked up at all"

Unfortunately, the massive network effects of smartphone ownership make it impractical for most people to simply do without. This fact is illustrated a somewhat funny, somewhat sad [story about director Werner Herzog being forced to buy a smartphone](https://www.pajiba.com/news/werner-herzog-had-to-get-a-cellphone-because-the-future-is-terrible.php) because he got stuck a parking garage without an electronic payment method. So, if we need a phone for basic activities of daily living, how can we still capitalize on phone-cleanse benefits? Could regular meditation provide some of the same mental benefits without having to ditch the phone? Can it be protective against negative some of the negative effects of regular smartphone use? Probably yes, and for minimal effort.
