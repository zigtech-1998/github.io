This [working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34788) (linked in the [Marginal Revolution blog](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/02/the-cocaine-problem-seems-to-be-getting-worse-again.html)) frames Colombian cocaine production in the context of American cost in both $ and human life. The paper finds a alarmingly strong relationship between the Colombian coca production and American cocaine overdoses:

> we find that the surge in supply caused an immediate rise in overdose mortality in the U.S. Our analysis estimates on the order of 1,000–1,500 additional U.S. deaths per year in the late 2010s can be attributed to Colombia’s cocaine boom. Implicit annual loss in American statistical life values about $48,000 per hectare of cultivation in Colombia. If left untamed, the current level of coca cultivation (over 230,000 ha in 2022) may impose on the order of $10 billion per year in costs via overdose fatalities.

On one hand, I understand why blame should be placed on individuals for cocaine overdose deaths. If you don't want to overdose on cocaine, don't do cocaine. We should also do a better job limiting domestic demand and preventing Cocaine from ever crossing American borders, regardless of how much cocaine foreign countries are making.

On the other hand, I understand there are some environmental and economic factors beyond an individual's control that would put them at higher risk for cocaine use. More importantly, if we find that Colombian cocaine production imposes an cost of ~1500 deaths and $10 billion dollars on the U.S., foreign drug production interdiction seems a justified & necessary piece of an effective homeland security plan.
