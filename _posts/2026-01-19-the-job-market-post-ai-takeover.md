Given the recent exponential growth in AI capability, it might not be long before the current human job market is winnowed down to occupations very similar to Tommy Norris' job in [Landman](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7zxh49-bsIk). AI, if subject to Moore's law, will more than likely become better at flying, driving, engineering, doctoring, lawyering, and many other currently coveted jobs than most humans very soon. It is already better than most humans at coding and some other cerebral white collar work. Eventually, the only jobs left from our current economy could be those requiring a physical presence and a whit without internet. And those jobs are few and far between. At least, that's how it appears from my very limited vantage point.

I promise, I'm not an AI doomer. As with every new advancement in technology, existing jobs are disrupted while entire new (and often more desirable) sectors are created. When the Model T was released, it certainly put a lot of street cleaners and horse feeders out of business. However, it generated factory jobs, car maintenance jobs, public infrastructure jobs, etc etc... There's no way anybody could have predicted these new jobs on Model T release date, but the world is certainly better off for it. I think the same is probably true for AI, but that might not make today's uncertainty any less uncomfortable.
