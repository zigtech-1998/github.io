First, I should clarify that by "digital productivity tools" I'm mostly referring to email, instant messaging, task management apps, and other digitized 'hacks' that promise step-function increases in knowledge work productivity. These things are talked about constantly and command a massive amount of capital in our digital economy. The Gemini summary from a quick Google search on the current digital productivity market showed the following:

>**Key Findings on Productivity Apps (2025–2026):**
>
> - **Market Size & Revenue:** The global productivity apps market was valued at over **$13 billion in 2025** and is projected to reach over **$30 billion by 2034**. Productivity apps generated over $30 billion in revenue in 2024, an increase of 17.3%.
> - **AI Dominance:** AI-powered productivity apps are dominating the market, with ChatGPT alone accumulating over 845 million downloads in 2025. AI app revenue was $4.5 billion in 2024, expected to more than triple in 2025.
> - **Investor Capital:** Investor focus has shifted heavily to AI-driven tools. While total AI investments for 2025 are projected to add another $375 billion, specific investments in new AI-native office suites and productivity tools are significant. For example, AI coding tools alone saw a surge to $4.0 billion in 2025, and companies like [Notion](https://www.notion.com/) have reached over 100 million users.

Yeah, a lot of this is driven by the huge AI and "AI-enabled application" craze. But what does AI-enabled mean? Is it actually making us better? I'd still consider myself an AI optimist, but the jury's still out on whether today's "AI-enabled" productivity tools are actually making us more productive, or whether they're just making us busier. This is a complicated question I won't attempt to answer today. Instead, I’ll try tackling a much simpler one: Do we really need these productivity tools to build great things? 

I think the clear answer to that question is: **No**.

I often wonder if the concept of 'productivity hacking' even crossed knowledge worker's minds in the 1960s... would the concept of 'productivity maxxing' have been completely foreign to them? Before the distraction of constant frictionless communication, it was likely much simpler to manage your professional workflow. Without the requirement of taming ad-hoc IMs and ever-increasing email junk, it was probably much easier to sit down and *just do actual work*. Amazing things were accomplished without these digital tools we now seem to think 'essential'. A short, non-exhaustive list of all the amazing shit American science accomplished prior to the advent of email or the internet:
1. Landed man on the moon and returned him safely to earth
2. Built the SR-71 blackbird
3. Built the MRI
4. Built an atomic bomb (obviously not amazing considering it killed a ton of people, but it's an undeniable scientific achievement).

Turns out you don't need a bunch of fancy productivity technology or instant communication to build great things[^1]. Not only are these tools not required - they may be net detractors from both productivity and workplace morale. 

An excerpt from a recent Cal Newport blog post, called [*In Defense of Thinking*](https://calnewport.com/in-defense-of-thinking-2/), isolates two great strategies for moving away from the hyperactive digital default:

> - Build work cultures in which phones and laptops stay out of meetings, and find collaboration strategies that don't require constant messaging.
> - Stop vague demands to "use AI" and instead carefully integrate these tools where they actually make us smarter, not just busier.

Fostering a work culture which bans laptops and phones in meetings would (a) make the meetings actually productive by increasing actual stakeholder engagement, and (b) would reduce the time wasted in meetings. So often in meetings I see *literally* everyone besides the speaker multi-tasking and not paying any attention. First - what is the damn point of having this meeting if nobody is paying attention? It's very expensive to get everyone in the same room for an hour. Why are we all here if the content is truly so meaningless that literally nobody feels the need to pay attention? Second - there usually isn't a real need for all this multitasking. Too often I justify this multitasking to myself - "I have too much going on to *not* multitask right now. I'm just exceptionally busy. Anybody who would frown upon this multitasking activity just doesn't get it." But that's a lie, and I know it. Before digital computers and frictionless, instant communication, meeting attendees had literally no choice but to pay attention. And they still got crazy shit done (see above).  If people know they cannot multitask, they are much more apt to skip or decline useless meetings. At minimum, they are much more likely to be prepared for making decisions and solving problems in the allotted time, lest another painful, computer-free follow-up be required.

As for the demands to integrate AI...
It is unsurprising and perhaps typical of the executive class to issue vague demands for their subordinates to utilize these new state-of-the-art tools so they can project a progressive image within their organization. Performative optics aside, there is little rigorous analysis of actual productivity gains around these tools. Just a bunch of non-technical leaders breathlessly asking their tech teams to 'do more' with AI and demonstrating progress with back-of-the-envelope efficiency estimations and use-case examples documented in an excel chart. AI is great at many things, and is certainly much better than humans at specific tasks. But forcing knowledge workers to use AI for tasks it can't yet do well and calling it 'efficiency maxxing' isn't helping anyone. Nevertheless, today's leadership class is still shouting 'do more! just do more!' without any real understanding of what it takes to solve hard technical problems. Maybe one day AI will make human thinking obsolete, but today the fact remains that the key driver for innovation (and the company's bottom line) is the cognitive output produced from their best & brightest through uninterrupted, focused struggling with hard problems using their very own noggin.
