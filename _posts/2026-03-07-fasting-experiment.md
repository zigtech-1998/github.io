Decided to do a little self-experimentation and fast for 48hrs about a week ago. I only made it 41 hours before breaking the fast with a single banana before an upper body workout. Simply getting out of my car in the gym parking lot made me feel so weak that I caved and ate something before tossing around some iron.[^1]

Couple interesting things I learned:
1. I always got super hungry around lunch and especially at dinner time. This is probably not news to people who are familiar with fasting, but these extreme hunger episodes were the most difficult part. But, they eventually subsided and I felt pretty much normal outside of my normal breakfast/lunch/dinner eating windows.
2. Sleep was fine the first night. Actually slept extremely well. However, towards the end of the second day I noticed a slight increase in RHR and a decrease in HRV. My sleep score (and subjective sleep quality) also decreased on night 2 (Thursday night). I initially fell asleep without any major issues but woke up twice during the night - once with a supreme headache - and had trouble going back to sleep.
		![image](/github.io/assets/images/fasting-experiment-pasted-image-20260301182634.png)
3. Definitely experienced some mental clarity benefits during both days during the fast. However, as 4pm approached, i found myself getting hungry, irritable, and distracted with thoughts of food. Outside of normal eating times I felt sharp, but around meal times I became extremely distracted with hunger.
4. Salt water and black coffee were key. Coffee kept me sharp and staved off hunger in the morning. Salt kept the "keto flu" away. I had tried fasting for 36hrs once in college with no electrolyte supplementation and it was miserable. Felt much better this time around with sodium. In the future I will probably add potassium and magnesium to the mix (maybe low potassium was a contributor to my elevated RHR later in the fast?).
5. Workouts felt extremely difficult and my performance suffered significantly. On Day 1, I had a 5 mile treadmill run planned, with 2 miles at tempo pace. Made it only 4.5 miles with about 1.25-1.5 miles at tempo pace. Heart rate at normally easy and tempo paces was through the roof, and perceived exertion was high: 
		![image](/github.io/assets/images/fasting-experiment-pasted-image-20260301182126.png)
		![image](/github.io/assets/images/fasting-experiment-pasted-image-20260301182223.png)
	Weight training on day 2 (right after I broke the fast with the banana) felt equally weak. I guess that's to be expected when there's no real fuel on board.
6. I did notice general mood improvements outside of normal eating windows, and my cravings reset to focus on healthier foods (blueberries, bananas + PB toast) rather than the junk foods I had been craving recently (Reese's, Snickers, ice cream, etc). 
7. Banana + PB toast + honey after 41+ hours of almost no food was the best damn meal I've ever had. Hunger really is the best sauce.

Overall a positive learning experience. I plan to do an extended 36-72hr fast probably 3-4 times a year, and a 24hr fast a 1-2 times per month. The metabolic reset and mental clarity are definitely worth it, and the mental challenge brings additional benefits (even though it's not very pleasant). 

[^1]:"Tossing" is a pretty generous term. I was shaking like a leaf on dumbbell bench press and fighting for my life to avoid dropping the bells directly on my head (which was consumed exclusively with thoughts of banana bread). Was a very shaky workout, but bodyweight dips were moving fairly well towards the end (probably as the glucose from the banana entered my bloodstream). Just happy to get it done without passing out.
