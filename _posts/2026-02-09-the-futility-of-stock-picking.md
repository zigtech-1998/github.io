Maybe the best first-principles example of why stock picking is impossible can be found in two of the biggest, most profitable businesses of our time. 

Google, who has the single greatest business model in history, didn't initially know how to monetize their own product.

Similarly, Microsoft had some initial struggles optimizing monetization for their software products. 

Both required supporting technologies and ecosystems to mature before their business models fully crystallized and before they could really start capitalizing with incredibly high margins.

If the inventors of the tech themselves didn't know how to properly monetize their business at the start, how can institutional investors, and especially private investors, reliably pick which stocks will skyrocket and which will flop? It's all just dumb luck. The only reliable truth is that the market as a whole will find a way to produce winners over a long timescale. Which is why indexing works.

(Caveat: the market will always find a way provided it is uninhibited by government regulations which stifle innovation or create sinister incentives)
